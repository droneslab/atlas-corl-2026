Overview

Active Sensing and Learning for Robot Perception brings together researchers working on adaptive sensing policies, task-grounded perception, controllable acquisition, multimodal robot sensing, computational imaging, and robot learning.

Robots are increasingly deployed in complex real-world environments, where reliable operation depends on both understanding the surroundings and choosing appropriate actions. This workshop focuses on active perception: robots deliberately choosing sensing actions to improve their understanding of the environment.

The workshop views active perception through three complementary areas: computational imaging, generative AI, and embodied decision making. Across these areas, controllable acquisition is itself a form of robot intelligence, shaping what information is captured, how it is captured, and what ambiguities remain before downstream perception begins.

Scope

The workshop targets the following open research challenges:

  • Active image formation for embodied tasks: adaptive exposure, gain, HDR, illumination intensity, illumination direction, optical coding, and sensor readout for VIO, SLAM, navigation, manipulation, and inspection.
  • Active sensing formation beyond cameras: adaptive field-of-view control, scan allocation, zooming LiDAR, mmWave beamforming, acoustic probing, tactile exploration, event-camera triggering, and multimodal acquisition policies.
  • Counterfactual models of image-formation and sensing actions: generative or physics-aware models that predict what a robot would observe under alternate exposure, gain, lighting, coded capture, scan density, radar beam pattern, waveform, modality, or viewpoint choices.
  • Policies for choosing how to sense: learning and control methods that optimize sensing parameters for task success, uncertainty reduction, latency, energy, safety, or information gain.
  • Task-grounded evaluation and artifacts: robot benchmarks, datasets, demos, failure analyses, and metrics connecting acquisition choices to navigation, SLAM, manipulation, exploration, or safety outcomes.

Contributions on image enhancement, passive perception, broad foundation models, or generative imagery are welcome when explicitly grounded in a robot sensing or control loop where acquisition choices are part of the system.

Active Illumination Benchmark

The workshop will introduce an Active Illumination Benchmark built around a photorealistic simulation environment where a robot follows fixed, pre-programmed trajectories through challenging visual scenes.

Participants will design algorithms that control a constrained robot-mounted light source, including its intensity and direction, while the robot executes each trajectory. The setup isolates the sensing control problem: participants cannot change the robot motion or perception backend, but can decide how visual measurements should be formed through illumination.

The benchmark will be released as an open community resource with fixed trajectories and an open evaluation pipeline based on downstream SLAM performance, with results discussed collectively during the workshop rather than treated as a ranked competition.

Call for Papers

We invite 1-2 page extended abstracts, excluding references, on research directions that fit the workshop scope.

Submissions may include completed research, preliminary results, position papers, datasets, benchmarks, system demonstrations, negative results, or challenge statements.

All submissions will be non-archival. Accepted papers will be presented as posters, and a subset will be selected for lightning talks based on fit, reviewer feedback, and the workshop program.

Contributions should make clear how sensing is treated as an action space for a robot, embodied agent, or task-grounded perception system.

Submission deadlines and the submission link will be added once finalized.
